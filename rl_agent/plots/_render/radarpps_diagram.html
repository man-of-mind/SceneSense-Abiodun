Radar density (points/sec) enriches the input & accuracy — not the payload

Why payload is flat: however many radar points arrive, they are rasterized onto a fixed 4 × H × W grid (occupancy, inverse-range, velocity, stationary-age), and the backbone emits a fixed-shape feature tensor regardless. So higher pps means a denser, more informative input — not more bytes on the wire. Effect: pedestrian detection improves with radar density up to ~200k pps, then saturates; vehicles/segmentation are already saturated. More sensor data → better accuracy at constant payload.
